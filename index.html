Research

I work in the Distributed Computing Systems & Cybersecurity Lab (DiSCS) at the Institute of Computer Science, FORTH, in Heraklion. My interests are DNS security, web security, privacy and Internet measurements.

I'm currently working on the following studies:

  • Compression-based detection of suspicious bulk domain registrations

    A detection method that identifies compressible clusters of lexically similar newly registered domains.

  • Tracking pixels at web scale

    A collaborative measurement study across the Tranco top one million sites and Common Crawl archives. We study how widely two third-party tracking pixels are adopted, how sites configure them and how much personal data they collect automatically.

  • Malicious advertising on social media

    A collaborative study of a major social media platform's advertising ecosystem. We examine malicious ads, the advertisers behind them and potential gaps in platform policy.

Publications

COBRA: A Content-Agnostic Framework for Zero-Day Detection of Suspicious Domains

Fourtounis, A., Papadogiannakis, E., Papadopoulos, P., Kourtellis, N. and Markatos, E. (2026). In Proceedings of the 23rd International Conference on Security and Cryptography (SECRYPT), Volume 1.

  • DOI 10.5220/0015000800004103

COBRA flags suspicious domains without looking at their content. It clusters newly registered domains by lexical and structural similarity and identifies suspicious bulk domain registrations. Over three months it processed 1.5 million domains across six top-level domains and flagged more than 47,000 as suspicious. It reached 98.5% precision in evaluation and reduced detection delay by up to seven days.

Characterizing Suspicious Domain Registrations at Scale: Design of Content-Agnostic Zero-Day Domain Detection

Selected projects

  • PIIxel Leaks reproduction and extension

    May 2026 to Jul 2026

    Graduate team project. Code on GitHub

    Measured Meta Pixel adoption and personal-data collection across two Tranco snapshots and a set of illicit domains. We extended the original crawler to find hidden pixel deployments. Malicious domains that share pixel IDs were clustered, and their hosting and registrars were checked for signs of coordinated infrastructure.

  • Research paper reproductions

    2025 to 2026

    Graduate projects. Reports on GitHub

    Reproduced experiments on cloud performance tuning on my own and worked with others on reproductions of hash-table performance studies. Experiments ran on AWS and Azure, with a technical report for each.

  • Adversarial training via min-max optimization

    Jan 2026 to Feb 2026

    Graduate team project. Code on GitHub

    PGD-based adversarial training on CIFAR-10. The team's models reached 2.4 times the baseline accuracy under PGD attack and gained 7.37 points of clean accuracy. We studied the effect of pre-training, learning rates and the ratio of adversarial to clean samples. We presented the results at a poster session.

  • Cryptographic vulnerability analysis framework

    Nov 2024 to Dec 2024

    Team project. Code on GitHub

    A prototype that finds insecure cryptography in Python, Java and C code. It identifies weak primitives, unsafe configurations and hardcoded keys. It tracks findings in Flask and SQLite, suggests or applies fixes, re-scans and reports on remediation to help plan a move to post-quantum cryptography.

  • wGrep

    Jul 2024 to Aug 2024

    A Python toolkit that scans Common Crawl WARC records with configurable regular expressions. It splits scans across subprocesses and aggregates statistics by URL, domain and match.
